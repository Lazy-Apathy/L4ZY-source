# Explanation of each option, shown when the mouse rests on it in the settings
# menus (Options > Display > Advanced > Menus: "Explanation after", default 1.5 s).
#
#   KEY: text                  what the option does (short, English, for players)
#   cost KEY: Level - detail   what it costs (Level: None, Low, Medium, High, Very high)
#
# KEY is one of:
#   variable                   a setting: hwrtsoftshadows
#   variable=value             one choice of it (a radio button): hwrtsoftshadowrays=8
#   @Label                     a button, or the name written before a row of controls
#   >action                    what a button runs: >showgui ammobar ; a final * matches the
#                              start: ">screenres *" for every screen size button
#   #keyfield / #field         general line added to every key field / text field
# A label or action with a colon is quoted: @"Demo: X-ray": text
# Costs: per frame, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, 2560x1440 unless written otherwise; "measured" =
# from the measurement lots, "estimated" = no measurement (the classic options use the
# game's own speed colours). The source of each measured cost is listed by the lot that adds it.
# /settingsearchreload reads this file again. menuhelptext VAR [VALUE] shows a text.

# --- general lines ---
#keyfield: Click it, press the key or keys (several are fine), then Esc. Empty = no key.

# --- Options page ---
>showgui "display": Window or fullscreen, resolution, field of view, brightness, V-Sync and latency.
>showgui "gfx": Lighting (classic or ray tracing), anti-aliasing, shadows, performance profiles, HDR screen.
>showgui "audio": Volumes, sound mix, 3D sound and the sound device.
>showgui "mouse": Sensitivity, DPI, cm/360 and the menu cursor speed.
>showgui "keys": Every key of the game: click a field and press the keys.
>showgui "hud": What is shown on screen while you play.
>showgui "friends": Friends by nick or clan tag, and the colours of teammates, enemies and friends.
>showgui "traduction": Translation of the chat, the AI model and the commands.
>showgui "console": The console, the chat box and the full-screen chat.
>showgui "record": Match videos, demos with HD aim, and demo playback.
@Search settings: Find any setting by a word, in English or French (sound, souris, shadows...).
@Settings assistant (AI): Ask the local AI how to change a setting. Nothing changes until you click Apply.
@Import settings from another client: Copy keys, name, options, servers and friends from Sauerbraten or an older L4ZY.
@Edit autoexec.cfg: Your own commands, run at every start.
@Restore defaults (clears all settings): Puts every setting back to its default. You are asked to confirm.
@Yes, restore defaults: Clears all your settings now (keys, graphics, sound, HUD...).

# --- Graphics > General ---
hwrt: The lighting of the game.
hwrt=0: The original Sauerbraten lighting. Fastest, any graphics card. Its settings: Classic tab.
hwrt=1: Ray-traced lamps, sun, sky light, soft shadows, reflections and weapon lights. Needs a graphics card with hardware ray tracing and an up-to-date driver. Its settings: Ray tracing tab.
cost hwrt=0: None - the fastest lighting
cost hwrt=1: Very high - about 3 to 8 ms more per frame than Classic (measured, Native)
lookfxaa: Softens the edges in Native, but also the textures. Off (default) keeps Native sharp. Not used with DLAA, DLSS or FSR.
cost lookfxaa: Low - a light full-screen filter (estimated)
fsaa: Smooth edges, sharp textures (the original game's anti-aliasing). Classic lighting with Native only. A change restarts the graphics.
cost fsaa: Medium - more with 4x and 8x (estimated)
hwrtngxmode: Anti-aliasing and upscaling. Native = every pixel drawn. Quality = more internal pixels: sharper, slower.
hwrtngxmode=0: Native: every pixel drawn by the game, no AI.
hwrtngxmode=1: DLAA: NVIDIA AI anti-aliasing at full resolution, the best picture. NVIDIA RTX only.
hwrtngxmode=2: DLSS Quality: fewer pixels drawn, rebuilt by the AI. Faster, nearly as sharp.
hwrtngxmode=3: DLSS Balanced: fewer pixels than Quality. Faster, a bit softer.
hwrtngxmode=4: DLSS Performance: half the pixels in each direction. Fastest DLSS, softest.
hwrtngxmode=5: FSR Native: AMD's open anti-aliasing at full resolution. Any graphics card.
hwrtngxmode=6: FSR Quality: fewer pixels drawn, rebuilt by FSR. Faster, nearly as sharp.
hwrtngxmode=7: FSR Balanced: fewer pixels than Quality. Faster, a bit softer.
hwrtngxmode=8: FSR Performance: half the pixels in each direction. Fastest, softest.
cost hwrtngxmode=0: None - the reference
cost hwrtngxmode=1: High - about 1.4 to 1.5 ms more than Native (measured, ray tracing)
cost hwrtngxmode=2: None - faster than Native: 0.5 to 2.8 ms less (measured, ray tracing)
cost hwrtngxmode=3: None - faster than DLSS Quality (estimated)
cost hwrtngxmode=4: None - the fastest DLSS (estimated)
cost hwrtngxmode=5: Medium - the FSR pass alone takes 0.5 to 0.9 ms (measured)
cost hwrtngxmode=6: None - faster than Native (estimated)
cost hwrtngxmode=7: None - faster than FSR Quality (estimated)
cost hwrtngxmode=8: None - the fastest FSR (estimated)
hwrtfsrsharpness: Extra sharpening after FSR upscaling. 0 = off, 10 = about like DLSS.
cost hwrtfsrsharpness: Low (estimated)
hwrtdlsssharpness: Sharpening after DLAA or DLSS (AMD's open filter). 20 is crisper; higher can show bright or dark specks. Not in native HDR.
cost hwrtdlsssharpness: Low (estimated)
hwrtupscalemipbias: With DLSS or FSR Quality / Balanced / Performance: textures as detailed as the screen resolution. Can shimmer a bit more in motion.
cost hwrtupscalemipbias: None (estimated)
fullbrightmodels: Players are lit whatever the light of the map, easier to see.
forceplayermodels: Everyone is shown with your player model.
teamskins: Blue (your team) and red (others) skins, even outside team games.
blood: Blood when players are hit.
hidedead: Dead players disappear at once.
ragdoll: Bodies fall with physics.
ragdollmillis: Dead bodies stay a while after the player respawns.
@Ragdoll push (velocity multiplier): How far explosions push dead bodies.
deadpush: How far explosions push dead bodies.

# --- Graphics > Ray tracing ---
hwrtteleportlight: Teleporters glow and light the room around them.
cost hwrtteleportlight: Low (estimated)
hwrtjumppadlight: Jump pads glow and light the room around them.
cost hwrtjumppadlight: Low (estimated)
hwrtdynlights: Muzzle flashes, rockets, grenades and explosions light the walls, floor, players and your gun. Custom trail colours tint the light.
cost hwrtdynlights: Low - nothing when nobody shoots, up to 0.34 ms in a fight (measured)
hwrtlampsall: Every lamp of the map lights, with the mapper's spotlights in their cone, the lava and the glowing panels; no light turns on or off as you move.
hwrtlampsall=0: Only the first 256 lamps of a map light, without spotlights, lava or glowing panels (as before).

# --- Path tracing (Graphics > General > Lighting, Graphics > Path tracing) ---
GFX_light: How the game is lit.
GFX_light=0: Classic: the original Sauerbraten lighting. Fastest, any graphics card.
GFX_light=1: Ray tracing: ray-traced lamps, sun and sky light, shadows and reflections. Needs a graphics card with hardware ray tracing.
GFX_light=2: Path tracing: the ray tracing plus the light that bounces off walls, floors and ceilings into the shade. Needs a graphics card with hardware ray tracing.
cost GFX_light=2: High - about 2 to 6.5 ms more than the ray tracing, 22 to 35 % fewer fps (measured, DLAA)
hwrtptlogical: Where the light comes from, in ray tracing and path tracing.
hwrtptlogical=0: Map lamps: the lights the mapper placed (default).
hwrtptlogical=1: Scene lights: the torches, light fittings, glowing textures, lava and teleporters you see light the room; the lamps the mapper placed are not used.
cost hwrtptlogical=1: Medium - from 0.9 ms less to 3.4 ms more than the map lamps; most on maps full of glowing textures (measured, DLAA)
hwrtpttexpower: How strongly the glowing textures light the room (Scene lights). 1: as bright as they look.
hwrtpttexreach: How far the light of the glowing textures goes, in units (Scene lights). Farther lights more of the room and costs more.
hwrtpttexgroup: Glowing textures side by side, facing the same way, light as one (within this many units): much faster on maps with many glowing panels; a few steps away the light is the same. 0: each part lights on its own.
cost hwrtpttexgroup: None - it saves time: up to 6.7 ms on maps with many glowing textures (44 to 62 fps on one of them); nothing on the others (measured, DLAA)
hwrtptcombine: How the light of the sky, the lamps and the bounces add up.
hwrtptcombine=3: Physical: light from several sides adds up as real light does; shaded places are lit more (default).
hwrtptcombine=2: As before: the earlier way of adding them.
hwrtptbouncegain: How strongly light bounces off walls and floors into the shade. 1: as real light bounces (default); more lights the shade more.
hwrtptskypixel: Keeps the light steady while you move: a place that comes into view does not sparkle.
hwrtptskypixel=0: Bright specks for a moment where the view has just changed.
hwrtptskyis: One more ray per pixel toward the bright parts of the sky: steadier light near openings when you move.
cost hwrtptskyis: Medium - 0.7 to 1.8 ms (measured, DLAA)
hwrtptskyscale: Light from the sky in the shade. 0: none.
hwrtptskyforce: On maps with a sun, the sky lights the shade with the strength below. Off: the strength the map gives.
hwrtptskyratio: How much the sky lights the shade, as a share of the sun's light.
hwrtptskyhue=1: The light of the sky takes the colour the mapper chose, as in the classic lighting (default).
hwrtptskyhue=0: The light of the sky takes the colours of the sky picture (often bluer).
hwrtptglass: Light goes through the gaps of leaves and through see-through parts of models.
hwrtptglass=0: Leaves and see-through parts stop the bounced light. Faster on maps with trees.
cost hwrtptglass: Medium - up to 3 ms on maps with many trees (measured, DLAA)
hwrtptflames: The light of a torch follows its flame: it moves and flickers with it (Scene lights).
hwrtptflamehidden: A torch out of view keeps flickering the same way (Scene lights).
hwrtptlogicwindows: A lamp the mapper set behind a window lights the room (Scene lights). Off: the window lets the sky's light through.
hwrtshadowself: Which lights draw your own shadow in first person.
hwrtshadowself=0: No shadow of yourself.
hwrtshadowself=1: The sun only: the one that always looks right.
hwrtshadowself=2: The sun and glowing lights (teleporters, lava...).
hwrtshadowself=3: Also the nearest lamps: the shadow stretches and breaks up next to a lamp.
hwrtshadowself=4: Also the sky: a dark patch around you.
cost hwrtshadowself: Low (estimated)
hwrtshadowothers: Which lights draw the shadows of players, bots, rockets and pickups.
hwrtshadowothers=0: No shadows of the others.
hwrtshadowothers=1: Shadows from the sun and the sky.
hwrtshadowothers=2: Also from teleporters, lava and jump pads.
hwrtshadowothers=3: Also from the map's lamps. A hidden lamp can add a second shadow.
cost hwrtshadowothers: Low - up to 0.2 ms (measured)
cost hwrtshadowothers=3: Low - 0.2 to 0.3 ms more (measured)
hwrtsmokeshadow: Weapon smoke (rifle trails, rockets, grenades) casts soft shadows.
cost hwrtsmokeshadow: Low - nothing without smoke, up to 0.1 to 0.2 ms with a lot of smoke (measured)
hwrtsmokeshadowdensity: How dark the smoke shadows are, in percent (default 100).
hwrtsoftshadows: Shadows of the sun, lamps, torches and teleporters get soft edges that blur with distance, sharp at the contact.
hwrtsoftshadows=0: Sharp shadows. Faster.
hwrtsoftshadows=2: Soft shadows for the sun and the lamps.
cost hwrtsoftshadows: High - 1.1 to 2.5 ms, 17 to 25 % fewer fps (measured, DLAA)
cost hwrtsoftshadows=0: None
hwrtsoftshadowrays: Rays per light for soft shadows. More = truer, less grainy edges, slower.
hwrtsoftshadowrays=1: Fast, but grainy edges.
hwrtsoftshadowrays=2: Truer edges for a moderate cost.
hwrtsoftshadowrays=4: Nearly perfect edges.
hwrtsoftshadowrays=8: As good as it gets, very slow.
cost hwrtsoftshadowrays=1: Low - the base of soft shadows
cost hwrtsoftshadowrays=2: Medium - 0.2 to 1.2 ms more than 1 ray (measured)
cost hwrtsoftshadowrays=4: High - 1.3 to 3.4 ms more than 1 ray (measured)
cost hwrtsoftshadowrays=8: Very high - more than 4 rays (estimated)
hwrtsoftsigma: Smooths the soft shadow edges (NVIDIA's open NRD SIGMA denoiser): clean even with 1 ray. Grainy shadows: turn it on first. Needs the NRD denoiser.
cost hwrtsoftsigma: Medium - 0.65 to 1.3 ms (measured)
hwrtsoftsigmahistory: Frames the denoiser keeps. 0 follows motion at once but is grainier; 7 is the calmest. If shadows smear when you move, lower it.
cost hwrtsoftsigmahistory: Low - 0 is 0.1 to 0.4 ms faster than 5 (measured)
hwrtreflections: Sharp ray-traced reflections on water and reflective surfaces.
cost hwrtreflections: Low - 0 to 0.4 ms (measured, with the shine below)
hwrtwaterwaves: Animated waves: the RT reflections ripple like real water. The water stays flat for the game. Needs the reflections.
cost hwrtwaterwaves: Low - 0.05 to 0.15 ms (measured)
waterwaveamp: Strength of the animated waves (0.25 to 3, default 1). Shared by RT and classic water.
waterwavespeed: Speed of the animated waves (0.25 to 3, default 1). Shared by RT and classic water.
waterwavesize: Detail of the animated waves. Shared by RT and classic water.
cost waterwavesize: Low - about 0.06 ms whatever the size (measured)
hwrtspecular: Shine of the lamps and the sun on glossy surfaces.
cost hwrtspecular: Low - 0 to 0.4 ms (measured, with the reflections)
hwrtsmoothnormals: Smooth light across faceted floors, slopes and rocks, like classic lighting.
cost hwrtsmoothnormals: Low (estimated)

# --- Bots ---
botmatchcount: How many bots the match starts with.
cost botmatchcount: Low - more bots use more processor (estimated)
botmatchminskill: Skill of the weakest bot (bots get a skill between the minimum and the maximum).
botmatchmaxskill: Skill of the strongest bot.
botperception: How the bots play.
botperception=0: Classic: the normal Sauerbraten bots.
botperception=2: Tiny Apa (work in progress): bots modelled on one real player's play.
botstylelevel: How good the Tiny Apa bots are. 100 = a strong player, 50 = half the accuracy, slower.
botstyleform: Tiny Apa bots have a different form each game, as a real player does.
@form settings: How often Tiny Apa bots play tired, and their warm-up.
botstyletired: How often a game is played very tired (late at night): a bit less accurate and slower.
botstylewarmup: Tiny Apa bots are a little less accurate in the first two minutes. Off (default): their full level from the first second.
@shot settings: When Tiny Apa bots take their shot: how long they wait on you and how wide a shot they allow.
botstylepatience: Tiny Apa bots allow a wider shot the longer they wait on you: the time (ms) for that allowance to double.
botstylepatnear: Their wait only counts once their crosshair is near you (tenths of your half width). Off: from the moment they notice you, so more shots leave before they are on you.
botstyletolmax: The widest shot Tiny Apa bots allow out of a flick, in tenths of your half width. 0: no limit.
botstyleunpredtol: How much a target that keeps changing direction widens the shot they allow (%). Lower: they wait to be on you.
botstylereactduel: How much quicker Tiny Apa bots react to you in a duel than in team games (ms). Higher: a quicker first shot.
botstylereflexduel: In a duel, how much quicker Tiny Apa bots shoot when you appear right in front of them (ms). Higher: a quicker first shot.
botstylewallwait: Tiny Apa bots do not fire while their crosshair is on the wall in front of you. Off: they fire anyway.
botstyleaimspread: How far around you the point Tiny Apa bots aim at is drawn (%). Higher: more shots just beside you.
@attention settings: What Tiny Apa bots do with your sounds and with shots that pass close to them.
botstylefelt: Tiny Apa bots turn to where a shot that passed close to them came from, and leave a cover they hold for a shot or a sound elsewhere. Off: they keep watching where they last saw you.
botstyleheardwait: In a duel, how soon a sound of yours heard elsewhere makes Tiny Apa bots look there (ms after losing sight of you). 0: at once.
botstyleheardfar: In a duel, how far from where they lost you a sound of yours must be to make Tiny Apa bots look there (units). 0: any sound clearly elsewhere.
botnet: Tiny Apa bots take their steps, stops and jumps from a small network learned from one real player's recorded games (work in progress). Off: the rules, as before.
cost botnet: Low - about 0.001 to 0.003 ms of processor per bot and per frame (measured)
botnavgrid: Tiny Apa bots find their way on a walk grid the game makes of each map. Off: the map's waypoints, as before (none: they stand still).
cost botnavgrid: Low - the first time on a map, about 0.6 ms for about 12 s while the grid is made (measured)
@walk grid settings: How the walk grid is made and kept, and how varied the bots' ways are.
botnavsafe: Bots keep clear of death pits, lava and the void. Off: they can fall, as before.
navbuildshare: How much of each frame may be spent making the walk grid. More = ready sooner; on a processor-limited PC, fewer fps meanwhile.
cost navbuildshare: Low - up to about 10 % fewer fps while the grid is made, on a processor-limited PC (measured)
navcachemb: Disk space for the walk grids of the maps played lately. 0 = no cache: each map's grid is made again when loaded.
navvariety: 0 = always the shortest way; more = more varied ways.
@start match: Choose the mode and the map, then the bots join.

@Service Status: Writes in the console whether the translation service runs.
